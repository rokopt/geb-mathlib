**Goal:** a constructive interface by which a client specifies a finite
category by counts and a composition table, discharges associativity by
`rfl`, and obtains a mathlib `Category` at independent universe levels,
together with functor and 2-cell specifications carrying a strict
2-category structure, decidable equality and `Repr`.

**Architecture:** eight source modules in a forced dependency order.
`Basic.lean` states the specification type and its morphism arithmetic
with no imports at all; `Category.lean` generates the mathlib
`Category`; `FinCategory.lean` adds the diagonal `FinCategory` and is
the single allowlisted module; `Hom.lean` and `Hom2.lean` add the
1-cell and 2-cell specifications with their checkers and generated
mathlib data; `Bicategory.lean` assembles the strict 2-category;
`Decidable.lean` and `Repr.lean` add the decision and rendering
layers.

**Tech stack:** Lean 4 (`v4.33.0-rc1`), mathlib pinned at the same
`rev`, `lake`, `jj`.

**The spec is the specification.**
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-fin-cat-spec-design.md` records
which proof routes work, which fail and how, and why several
non-obvious choices are forced. Read the section named at the head of
each task before starting that task. Where this plan and the spec
disagree, the spec is authoritative and the disagreement is a defect in
this plan, except at the spec deviations § Deviations from the spec and
from the rules records as items 1, 2, 3 and 5; item 4 is a departure
from `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` rather than from the spec.

## Proof-detail convention

The spec deliberately does not pin tactic scripts. This plan is
calibrated accordingly, and every code block below carries one of three
labels so that no reader mistakes a sketch for a verified term.

- **Verified.** The block compiled on the pinned toolchain during
  planning. Reproduce it as written; if it fails, that is a
  toolchain-drift finding, not an occasion to substitute another proof.
- **Determined.** Data — a `def`'s body, a structure's fields, an
  instance's non-law fields — whose content is forced by the types.
  Write it as given. Implicit-argument counts and `▸` directions are
  the elaborator's to settle; adjust them without treating the change
  as a deviation.
- **Assigned.** A theorem or law field stated in full, with the
  inventory of *named* lemmas its proof consumes and the case
  structure identified, but no tactic script. Prove it with the
  `lean4:prove` workflow, escalating to `lean4:autoprove` and then
  `lean4:sorry-filler-deep`.

An inventory lists the named lemmas of this workstream and of mathlib
that carry the mathematical content. It does **not** list routine
machinery, which is always available without comment: unfolding a
definition of this workstream (`homCount`, `compTotal`, `mapTotal`,
`emb` and the rest), `Fin.ext` and `Fin.val` bookkeeping, `dif_pos` /
`dif_neg` / `Nat.not_lt` / `Nat.lt_irrefl` at a dispatch, `funext`,
`congrArg`, and `subst`. An **Assigned** block that needs a *named
mathematical* ingredient absent from its inventory is a finding against
the spec's § Verification obligations and is reported, not silently
patched.

Only three labels are used, and every Lean code block carries exactly
one. Import lists, index-registration lines, allowlist entries, shell
commands and `grep` invocations carry none.

**Test steps.** A test step's fixtures — the `def`s other steps refer
to by name — are given as code, at whichever of the three labels
applies. Its assertions are given either as code or as a numbered list
naming the statement, the fixture and the discharge (`rfl`, `decide`,
`#guard`, or a named theorem). The list form is still **Assigned**, but
what it must contain is the *statement*, not a description of one: an
assertion that cannot be written down from the step is a plan defect,
and two such defects — a test that could not compile and a test no
fixture could witness — survived into round 2 precisely because the
step described an intention instead of naming a statement.

## Global constraints

Every task's requirements implicitly include this section.

- **No `noncomputable` anywhere.** No `native_decide`:
  `GebMeta.detectNonstandardAxiom` forbids `Lean.ofReduceBool`
  everywhere.
- **Choice-free except one module.** Every declaration of every task
  depends on `propext` and `Quot.sound` only. Only
  `Geb.Mathlib.CategoryTheory.FinCat.FinCategory` and
  `GebTests.Mathlib.CategoryTheory.FinCat.FinCategory` are appended to
  `GebMeta.classicalAllowedModules`, and those two only. In
  particular no allowlist entry is planned for
  `FinCat/Bicategory.lean`, `FinCat/Decidable.lean` or
  `FinCat/Repr.lean`.
- **The checkers resolve through `Nat.decidableForallFin`.** Neither
  `Fintype.decidableForallFintype` nor `Fintype.decidablePiFintype`
  nor `Geb.Mathlib.Data.FinEnum`'s
  `FinEnum.decidableForallFinEnum` is used anywhere: the first two
  depend on `Classical.choice`, and the third needs
  `FinEnum (Fin n)`, whose only instance is choice-dependent. This
  workstream adds nothing to `Geb/Mathlib/Data/FinEnum.lean`.
- **`DecidableEq` arguments are supplied explicitly at every use
  site** in `Decidable.lean`, and `FinCat.decidableEqPiFin` is
  `scoped`. Both mitigations are required together: mathlib's
  `Fintype.decidablePiFintype` is a competitor at the same head
  symbol and is what default resolution selects in its absence.
- **No `induction` tactic, no self-recursive `def`, no
  `termination_by`.** Every recursion is an explicit recursor
  application, per `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` § Recursion and
  induction through recursors. `cases`, `rcases`, `obtain`,
  `by_cases` and `subst` are permitted for non-recursive case
  analysis.
- **Module preamble.** Copyright block
  (`Copyright (c) 2026 Terence Rokop. All rights reserved.`, the
  Apache-2.0 line, `Authors: Terence Rokop`), then `module`, then the
  imports, then the `/-! … -/` module docstring, then
  `@[expose] public section`, then `open CategoryTheory` in every
  module from `Category.lean` on. `𝟙` and `≫` are scoped notation, and
  `Category`, `Functor`, `eqToHom`, `eqToIso`, `Bicategory` and
  `StrictBicategory.category` all live in that namespace;
  `Geb/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/FinSetSkel/Basic.lean` places the `open`
  after the `public section`, which is the form to copy. `Basic.lean`
  mentions no category and takes no `open`.
- **Import visibility.** Imports whose contents appear in a module's
  own statements are `public import`; source index files use
  `public import`; `GebTests` index files use plain `import` and
  `GebTests` content modules use `public import` for the module under
  test.
- **`autoImplicit = false`.** Every binder is declared.
- **Line length 100 characters** in `.lean` files; 80 in `.md` prose
  (`MD013`, tables and code blocks exempt).
- **`weak.warningAsError = true`.** A linter warning fails the build.
  `linter.unusedVariables` and `linter.unusedSimpArgs` are core's and
  run everywhere, including in `Basic.lean`, which imports nothing: the
  first rejects a named binder that is never referenced, the second a
  `simp` argument that fires nowhere. `dupNamespace` and the
  `linter.style.*` family are mathlib's and run only in modules that
  import mathlib. `dupNamespace` matters from
  `Category.lean` onwards and fires on non-consecutive repetition too,
  so `FinCat.Hom₂.FinCat.Hom.instCategory` is rejected.
  `linter.style.show` is a *tactic* linter and does not see a term-mode
  `show … from …`.
- **`Nat`, not `ℕ`, throughout.** `objCount : Nat` is declared in a
  module that cannot import mathlib, so `ℕ` is unavailable at the
  point the API is fixed, and the later modules follow it.
- **Unicode where mathlib uses it**: `↦`, `∀`, `→`, `⟨ ⟩`, `≤`, `≠`,
  `⟶`, `≫`, `𝟙`.
- **Universe parameters are bound on the declaration, not by a
  `universe` command.** Write `FinCat.Obj.{u}`,
  `FinCat.Obj.category.{v, u}`, `FinCat.Obj.finCategory.{u}`,
  `FinCat.Hom.toFunctor.{v, u}` and `FinCat.Hom₂.toNatTrans.{v, u}`.
  A `universe` command orders a declaration's parameters by the order
  of that command, so `universe u v` would make
  `FinCat.Obj.category.{v, u}` bind the *object* level to `v` — the
  reverse of what every use site means. Binding on the declaration
  fixes the order at the declaration and is measured to elaborate.
  It also keeps every level a declared name, which
  `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` § Structure and typeclass patterns
  requires (no auto-bound `u_1`-style variables).
- **`CategoryTheory.Functor` is written qualified when written with
  `@`.** Under `open CategoryTheory`, `@Functor` is overloaded against
  core's `Functor : (Type u → Type v) → Type _` and does not
  elaborate. Write `@CategoryTheory.Functor`. The `⥤` and unapplied
  `Functor` forms are unaffected.
- **Every named binder is used or is `_`.** `linter.unusedVariables`
  fails the build, so an instance field whose proof ignores its
  arguments writes `map_id _ :=`, not `map_id X :=`.
- **Namespace blocks open and close explicitly at every step.** A step
  that adds a declaration states which `namespace` block it goes in.
  `dupNamespace` fails the build, so a fully-qualified
  `instance FinCat.Hom.instCategory` written inside an open
  `namespace FinCat.Hom₂` is a build failure, not a style nit.
- **Docstrings are mandatory** for every `def`, `structure`,
  `instance`, every structure field and every theorem, in both the
  source and the test modules, and a `/-! … -/` module docstring with
  the sections `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` § Documentation makes
  mandatory — `## Tags` included — heads each file. No
  development-history references and no axiom celebration in any
  docstring.
- **No self-prefix leakage.** `Geb.Mathlib.` and `GebTests.Mathlib.`
  appear only in `^import` lines, never in a namespace, a declaration
  body, a docstring or a comment. `scripts/lint-imports.sh` enforces
  this and admits only `Mathlib.`, `Batteries.` and `Geb.Mathlib.`
  import prefixes in this subtree.
- **Validity fields and arithmetic reduction assertions use `rfl`, not
  the `decide` tactic.** § Verification obligations item 2 measures that
  `assocCheck` reduces in the kernel, so `assoc := rfl` and
  `homCount … = 1 := rfl` are the idiom.
- **`Repr` assertions use `#guard`, not `rfl` and not `decide`.**
  `reprStr` routes through `Std.Format.prettyM`, which does not reduce
  in the kernel: `reprStr x = "…" := rfl` fails with a type mismatch and
  `by decide` reports reduction stuck at `prettyM`, both measured, even
  when the expected string is exactly right. `#guard` evaluates instead
  of reducing and is the only available idiom, `native_decide` being
  banned. `linter.hashCommand` does flag it — its allow-list is
  `{#adaptation_note}` alone — but because `weak.warningAsError = true`
  the linter logs an info rather than a warning, so the build does not
  fail. `GebTests/Internal/AxiomLinter.lean` already uses `#guard` nine
  times on that footing.
- **Object indices in tests are named `Fin`-typed definitions, not
  numerals.** `walkingArrow.homCount 0 1` does not elaborate:
  `walkingArrow.objCount` is a projection rather than a literal, so
  numeral elaboration finds no
  `OfNat (Fin walkingArrow.objCount) 0` instance. Each test fixture
  gets named indices — `def arrowSrc : Fin walkingArrow.objCount :=
  ⟨0, by decide⟩` — and assertions are written over those.
- **Disclosure.** This is LLM-drafted content in an upstream-eligible
  subtree, so CONTRIBUTING § Submission policy applies: before the
  user commits it they understand every line and can justify each
  decision without AI assistance, disclosure of tools used is
  mandatory, and a PR carrying a substantial amount of it carries the
  `LLM-generated` label. The PR description and any Zulip or GitHub
  comment are user-authored; AGENTS.md forbids an agent drafting them,
  and no task below produces any.
- **VCS is `jj`.** No mutating `git` subcommand; the PreToolUse hook
  at `scripts/hooks/block-mutating-git.sh` blocks them.
- **Commit messages**: `<type>(<scope>): <subject>`, type in
  `feat | fix | doc | style | refactor | test | chore | perf | ci`,
  imperative present tense, no capital, no trailing period, subject
  under 72 characters. Scope is `fin-cat`.
- **Every new module is registered in its index in the task that
  creates it.** Each task's Files table lists the index files it
  modifies; performing those edits is part of that task's final commit
  step, whether or not the step repeats them. `lake build` does not
  catch an omission — `lakefile.toml` globs `Geb.*` and `GebTests.*`,
  so an unindexed module still compiles — so the check is by reading
  `Geb/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/FinCat.lean` and its test parallel, which
  Task 15 Step 3 does once at the end.
- **Module import list per file is a starting point**, not
  `lake shake`'s output. The pre-push `lake shake` settles the
  minimal set; adjust imports to whatever it reports and re-run.

## Task order and the risk concentration

Three edges of the module order are forced: `Hom.lean`'s `toFunctor`
mentions `FinCat.Obj` and `FinCat.Obj.category`, so `Category.lean`
precedes it; `Hom2.lean`'s `toNatTrans` mentions two `toFunctor`s; and
`Bicategory.lean` consumes both. The rest is a linearisation, not a
forcing: `FinCategory.lean` is a leaf nothing consumes, and
`Decidable.lean` and `Repr.lean` depend on `Hom2.lean` rather than on
`Bicategory.lean`, so all three could move. Within that linearisation
the plan orders
the spec's five outstanding verification obligations, so that a
statement whose route is unproved is reached as early as its
dependencies allow:

| Outstanding obligation | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 2 — the `mapTotal` family | Tasks 6, 7, 9 |
| 3 — the strict equalities and the validity fields | Tasks 7, 9 |
| 4 — the ten coherence theorems | Task 11 |
| 1 — the `Bicategory` axiom figure | Task 12 |
| 5 — `DecidableEq` layers 2 and 3, and `Repr` | Tasks 13, 14 |

**The three bundled checkers each get one test consumer.**
`FinCat.assocCheck`, `FinCat.Hom.compCheck` and `FinCat.Hom₂.natCheck`
are the bundled wrappers the spec asks for so that its prose names
declarations that exist; every reflection lemma is stated over the
unbundled form, so nothing in the source consumes them. Rather than
leave three declarations with no consumer against CONTRIBUTING § Code
is cost, each is asserted `= true` by `rfl` on a fixture — `assocCheck`
in Task 3, `compCheck` in Task 7, `natCheck` in Task 9 — which also
checks that each wrapper applies its unbundled form to the right
fields.

`FinCat.Hom.mapTotal_compTotal` (Task 6) and the ten coherence
theorems (Task 11) are the risk concentration. Neither has a fallback
inside this workstream's scope: `FinCat.Hom.comp` cannot be defined
without the first, and the `Bicategory` instance cannot be assembled
without the second. If either resists, stop and report rather than
weakening a statement — `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` § Structure and
typeclass patterns forbids weakening the interface of a standard
concept to ease implementation.

## Deviations from the spec and from the rules

Five, each recorded here so no reviewer has to rediscover them. Items 4
and 5 are departures from a repository style rule and from the spec's
§ Testing rather than from its design.

1. **`FinCat.Hom.instCategory`'s three law fields get a discharge the
   spec does not assign.** § Verification obligations item 3 assigns
   the `natValid` fields of the identity 2-cell and the vertical
   composite, but not the instance's own `id_comp`, `comp_id` and
   `assoc` fields, which are equalities of `FinCat.Hom₂` values.
   mathlib's `Cat.Hom.instCategory` discharges its counterparts by
   `congrArg Hom₂.ofNatTrans`, available because `Cat.Hom₂` is a
   one-field wrapper; § Functor and 2-cell specifications records that
   `FinCat.Hom` is not such a bundling, so that route is unavailable.
   Task 9 therefore states `FinCat.Hom₂.eq_of_app_eq` at
   `FinCat.Hom₂ F G` — before the instance, where the hom notation
   does not yet exist — and discharges the three fields from it. The
   spec's item 2, `FinCat.Hom₂.ext` at `F ⟶ G`, still follows the
   instance and is still what the ten coherence proofs use.
2. **The hom-level `DecidableEq` reuses mathlib's rather than deciding
   afresh.** § The generated mathlib category asks that `FinCat.Obj`
   and the generated hom types carry `DecidableEq`, and both do:
   `FinCat.Obj` by `deriving`, the hom types by
   `FinCat.Obj.decidableEqHom`. The deviation is only in the body.
   A hom-level instance is unavoidable — measured, instance search does
   not unfold `Quiver.Hom`, so `DecidableEq (X ⟶ Y)` does not resolve
   from the `ULift` instance alone, as
   `Geb/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/FinSetSkel/Basic.lean`'s `decidableEqHom`
   also records — but its body is `inferInstanceAs` pointing at
   `Mathlib/Data/ULift.lean`'s instance over `Fin`'s, rather than a
   `decidable_of_iff` deciding the underlying data again. CONTRIBUTING
   § Code is cost prefers instantiating the existing abstraction, and a
   pointer cannot drift from what it points at.
3. **`FinCat.id_comp` and `FinCat.comp_id` take their index arguments
   implicitly.** § The checkers writes
   `FinCat.id_comp (S) (i k) (g : S.Mor i k)`. The indices are
   determined by `g`'s type and mathlib states `Category.id_comp` with
   them implicit; the plan follows mathlib. The abbreviation in the
   spec's rendering is read as binder-list shorthand rather than as a
   decision about explicitness.
4. **Five modules mix `namespace` blocks with top-level content.**
   `docs/rules/lean-coding.md` § Coding style says "do not mix
   `namespace X` blocks with content outside them in
   the same file". `Basic.lean`, `Hom.lean` and `Hom2.lean` each open
   their namespace, close it, declare a structure at top level, and
   reopen it. The departure is forced, not chosen: a
   structure named `FinCat` cannot be declared inside
   `namespace FinCat` without becoming `FinCat.FinCat`, and the
   structure's field types mention declarations that must therefore
   precede it inside the namespace. `Hom2.lean` mixes for a second
   forced reason — `FinCat.Hom.instCategory` is a `FinCat.Hom` name
   declared in a file whose other content is `FinCat.Hom₂`.
   `Bicategory.lean` and `Decidable.lean` mix for the same reason
   without declaring a structure: each holds `FinCat.*` instances that
   `dupNamespace` rejects inside the `FinCat.Hom₂` or `FinCat`
   namespace its other content occupies.
   `Geb/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/FinSetSkel/Basic.lean` has the same
   arrangement for the same reason.
5. **The tests are narrower than spec § Testing.** That section asks
   for the identity functor on each worked example, and for
   `DecidableEq` and `Repr` assertions on all four. The plan builds
   1-cells on `terminalCat`, `walkingArrow` and `idemMonoid`, uses
   `FinCat.Hom.id` on `walkingIso` only, decides on `walkingArrow`,
   `walkingIso` and the `idemMonoid` cells, and renders `walkingIso`,
   `FinCat.Hom.id walkingIso` and `idemCellIdem`. The omitted cases are
   repetitions at a different fixture rather than distinct behaviours —
   the identity
   1-cell's `compValid` discharges the same way at every specification —
   and CONTRIBUTING § Code is cost puts the burden on the marginal
   assertion. Coverage is chosen instead by what can fail: the two-element
   hom-set of `idemMonoid` carries every negative assertion, and
   `walkingIso` carries the reserved-index composite. Extend rather than
   argue if a later consumer wants the full grid.

```lean
/-!
# Finite-category specifications

A client specifies a finite category by a count of objects, a count of
non-identity morphisms at each pair of objects, a composition function
on those morphisms, and a `Bool` equation asserting associativity. The
identities are not the client's to supply: one is reserved at the index
one past the client's range in each endo-hom, so the identity laws hold
of the reserved index by construction and only associativity is
checked.

Composition takes the hom-count matrix as primitive rather than a total
morphism count with domain and codomain projections. Under the matrix
the indexing makes well-typedness a typing obligation, and composition
is total; under the projections it is partial, and the client and the
checker carry the two side conditions relating the endpoints of a
composite to those of its factors.

The identity is reserved one past the client's range rather than at
index `0` so that the embedding of a client morphism into the full hom
type is index-preserving both on and off the diagonal. Reserving `0`
makes the embedding `Fin.succ` on the diagonal and the identity map off
it, so a client's composition function would take arguments in one
numbering and return a result in another.

Total composition dispatches on whether either argument is the reserved
identity, which involves the equation `i = j` between objects. That
equation is used only inside a `Prop`: each branch returns
`⟨x.val, proof⟩`, so the value component crosses with no `Eq.rec` and
only the bound is transported. This is core's own idiom for
`Fin.castLE`.

This module has no imports. Its `Fin` and `Nat` material is in the
prelude, and nothing in its content mentions a category.

## Main definitions

* `FinCat` — the specification type.
* `FinCat.homCountOf`, `FinCat.homCount` — the hom-count including the
  reserved identity.
* `FinCat.Mor`, `FinCat.emb`, `FinCat.id` — the full hom type, the

* `FinCat.compTotalOf`, `FinCat.compTotal` — composition on the full
  hom types.
* `FinCat.assocCheckOf`, `FinCat.assocCheck` — the decidable
  associativity check on triples of client morphisms.

## Main statements

* `FinCat.id_comp`, `FinCat.comp_id` — the identity laws, at variable
  indices.
* `FinCat.assocCheck_eq_true_iff` — the check reflects associativity on
  client triples.
* `FinCat.compTotal_assoc` — associativity on all triples.
```

- [ ] **Step 5: prove them**

**Verified**, and measured **axiom-free** — which is what § Verification
obligations item 1 requires. Two reusable `homCountOf` lemmas come
first; they are what keep the proofs free of `simp`, whose closure
lemmas would pull in `propext` and `Quot.sound`. The hypotheses are
stated as `≤` rather than `¬ <` because mathlib prefers the former, and
the names relate the terms they mention rather than interpreting them.

```lean
/-- Off the diagonal the reserved identity contributes nothing. -/
theorem homCountOf_of_ne {objCount : Nat}
    {nonIdCount : Fin objCount → Fin objCount → Nat} {i j : Fin objCount} (hij : ¬ i = j) :
    homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i j = nonIdCount i j :=
  (congrArg (nonIdCount i j + ·) (if_neg hij)).trans (Nat.add_zero _)

/-- On the diagonal it contributes one. -/
theorem homCountOf_diag {objCount : Nat}
    {nonIdCount : Fin objCount → Fin objCount → Nat} (i : Fin objCount) :
    homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i i = nonIdCount i i + 1 :=
  congrArg (nonIdCount i i + ·) (if_pos rfl)

/-- An index at or beyond the client's count exists only on the
diagonal: off the diagonal the conditional in `homCountOf` contributes
`0`. -/
theorem objEq_of_le {objCount : Nat} {nonIdCount : Fin objCount → Fin objCount → Nat}
    {i j : Fin objCount} (x : Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i j))
    (h : nonIdCount i j ≤ x.val) : i = j :=
  if hij : i = j then hij
  else absurd (homCountOf_of_ne (nonIdCount := nonIdCount) hij ▸ x.isLt) (Nat.not_lt.mpr h)

/-- An index at or beyond the client's count is the reserved identity
index of its object. -/
theorem val_eq_of_le {objCount : Nat} {nonIdCount : Fin objCount → Fin objCount → Nat}
    {i j : Fin objCount} (x : Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i j))
    (h : nonIdCount i j ≤ x.val) : x.val = nonIdCount j j := by
  have hij := objEq_of_le x h
  subst hij
  exact Nat.le_antisymm
    (Nat.le_of_lt_succ (Nat.lt_of_lt_of_eq x.isLt (homCountOf_diag (nonIdCount := nonIdCount) i))) h
```

The two `homCountOf` lemmas are not scaffolding to be inlined: they are
the only place the conditional in `homCountOf` is analysed, and every
later proof that needs to know a hom-count reaches them rather than
unfolding the `if`.

```lean
/-- Composition on the full hom types, dispatching on whether either
argument is the reserved identity. Both elided bounds are
`objEq_of_le`: the value component crosses unchanged and only the
bound is transported. -/
def compTotalOf {objCount : Nat} {nonIdCount : Fin objCount → Fin objCount → Nat}
    (comp : (i j k : Fin objCount) → Fin (nonIdCount i j) → Fin (nonIdCount j k) →
      Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i k))
    {i j k : Fin objCount} (f : Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i j))
    (g : Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount j k)) :
    Fin (homCountOf objCount nonIdCount i k) :=
  if hf : f.val < nonIdCount i j then
    if hg : g.val < nonIdCount j k then comp i j k ⟨f.val, hf⟩ ⟨g.val, hg⟩
    else ⟨f.val, by
      have hjk := objEq_of_le g (Nat.not_lt.mp hg); subst hjk; exact f.isLt⟩
  else ⟨g.val, by
    have hij := objEq_of_le f (Nat.not_lt.mp hf); subst hij; exact g.isLt⟩
```

When both arguments are the reserved identity the outermost `else`
fires and returns the identity, which is correct. No
`attribute [irreducible]` is applied to this or to anything else in
the workstream: a seal would defeat the `rfl` discharge the design
rests on, `irreducible` blocking the elaborator's definitional
unfolding.

```lean
  compTotalOf S.comp f g
```

**Verified.** Neither is `rfl`: the dispatch in `compTotal` is a `dite`
on `f.val < S.nonIdCount i j`, a `Nat.decLt` at two variable naturals,

```lean
/-- The reserved identity is a left identity for the total
composition. -/
theorem id_comp (S : FinCat) {i k : Fin S.objCount} (g : S.Mor i k) :
    S.compTotal (S.id i) g = g := by
  have hlt : ¬ ((S.id i).val < S.nonIdCount i i) := Nat.lt_irrefl _
  unfold FinCat.compTotal compTotalOf
  rw [dif_neg hlt]
  rfl

/-- The reserved identity is a right identity for the total
composition. -/
theorem comp_id (S : FinCat) {i j : Fin S.objCount} (f : S.Mor i j) :
    S.compTotal f (S.id j) = f := by
  have hlt : ¬ ((S.id j).val < S.nonIdCount j j) := Nat.lt_irrefl _
  unfold FinCat.compTotal compTotalOf
  by_cases hf : f.val < S.nonIdCount i j
  · rw [dif_pos hf, dif_neg hlt]
    rfl
  · rw [dif_neg hf]
    exact Fin.ext (val_eq_of_le f (Nat.not_lt.mp hf)).symm
```

Two mechanics, both measured. `dif_neg (Nat.lt_irrefl _)`
inline does not fire: `(S.id i).val` is not syntactically
`S.nonIdCount i i`, and `FinCat.id` is semireducible, so `rw` finds no
match. Binding the hypothesis first, with `(S.id i).val` written out,
makes the pattern match and lets the elaborator discharge
`Nat.lt_irrefl _` up to defeq. And `rw` does not close the residual
`⟨g.val, _⟩ = g`, trying `rfl` only at reducible transparency; the
trailing `rfl` does, by structure eta and definitional proof
irrelevance.

```lean
type mentions only the fixed parameters `F` and `G`. -/
instance FinCat.Hom₂.decidableEq {S T : FinCat} {F G : FinCat.Hom S T} :
    DecidableEq (FinCat.Hom₂ F G) :=
  fun α β ↦
    match α, β with

      | isTrue h => isTrue (by subst h; rfl)
      | isFalse h => isFalse (fun e ↦ h (by injection e))

/-- Decidable equality of functor specifications: decide `objMap`,
transport along that equality — `map`'s type mentions `objMap` — then
decide `map`. -/
instance FinCat.Hom.decidableEq {S T : FinCat} : DecidableEq (FinCat.Hom S T) :=
  fun F G ↦
    match F, G with
    | ⟨o, m, _⟩, ⟨o', m', _⟩ =>
      match @FinCat.decidableEqPiFin _ _ (fun _ ↦ inferInstance) o o' with
      | isFalse h => isFalse (fun e ↦ h (by injection e))
      | isTrue ho => by
        subst ho
        exact match @FinCat.decidableEqPiFin _ _ (fun _ ↦
            @FinCat.decidableEqPiFin _ _ (fun _ ↦ inferInstance)) m m' with
          | isTrue h => isTrue (by subst h; rfl)
          | isFalse h => isFalse (fun e ↦ h (by injection e))
```

only candidate. The `Bool`-equation fields are proof-irrelevant, so
`subst h; rfl` closes each positive case and no field equality for them
appears; `injection` supplies the negative cases.

**Verified**, all three. Each renders the counts and the tables as
nested naturals, through `List.ofFn` and `Fin.val`. `List.ofFn` is
axiom-free and needs no import beyond core;
`Geb/Mathlib/Data/Vector/OfFn.lean` exists for choice-free *indexing

`public import Geb.Mathlib.CategoryTheory.FinCat.Hom2`.

```lean
/-- Renders a specification as its object count, its count matrix and
its composition table. -/
instance FinCat.instRepr : Repr FinCat where
  reprPrec S _ :=
    repr (S.objCount,
      List.ofFn (fun i ↦ List.ofFn (fun j ↦ S.nonIdCount i j)),
      List.ofFn (fun i ↦ List.ofFn (fun j ↦ List.ofFn (fun k ↦
        List.ofFn (fun f ↦ List.ofFn (fun g ↦ (S.comp i j k f g).val))))))

/-- Renders a functor specification as its object map and its morphism
table. -/
instance FinCat.Hom.instRepr {S T : FinCat} : Repr (FinCat.Hom S T) where
  reprPrec F _ :=
    repr (List.ofFn (fun i ↦ (F.objMap i).val),
      List.ofFn (fun i ↦ List.ofFn (fun j ↦ List.ofFn (fun f ↦ (F.map i j f).val))))

/-- Renders a 2-cell specification as its component vector. -/
instance FinCat.Hom₂.instRepr {S T : FinCat} {F G : FinCat.Hom S T} :
    Repr (FinCat.Hom₂ F G) where
  reprPrec α _ := repr (List.ofFn (fun i ↦ (α.app i).val))
```

Each drops to `Fin.val` at the leaves and to `List` at every index, so
the `Repr` instances reached are core's for `Nat`, `List` and tuples.
The `Bool`-validity fields are not rendered: they carry no information
a reader of the table needs, being determined by the table.

`Repr` requires no transport at any level: it maps out of the dependent
structure into `Format`, discarding the types whose disagreement
obstructs equality. That is why these three are a separate, simpler
module from Task 13's.
